# Why the Iran-Backed Houthis in Yemen and Saudi Arabia Are Back at War

The conflict has opened a second front in the Middle East after the United States and Israel attacked Iran, adding to global uncertainty and turmoil in energy markets.

After four years of relative peace, Yemen has spiraled back into full-blown war.

In a lightning offensive, the Iran-backed Houthi militia has seized control of the country’s Red Sea coastline in recent weeks. The group is now engaged in [fierce clashes with Saudi-backed, Yemeni government forces](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/09/20/world/middleeast/saudi-arabia-yemen-houthis-fighting.html) on multiple fronts, and cross-border attacks in Yemen and Saudi Arabia have escalated.

The war has opened a second front in a Middle East conflict that began when the United States and Israel attacked Iran in February, adding to regional volatility, sending energy prices soaring even farther and darkening the shadow over the global economy.

A year ago, Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman of Saudi Arabia, the country’s de facto ruler, and President Trump signed a strategic defense agreement. The Saudi leader has repeatedly asked Mr. Trump to intervene militarily against the Houthis, part of the so-called [axis of resistance](https://www.nytimes.com/article/axis-resistance-iran-militia.html) led by Iran, along with Hezbollah in Lebanon and Hamas in Gaza.
